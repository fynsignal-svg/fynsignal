Hospitality and heritage restoration Workflow automation

Supplier invoice automation

A heritage hospitality and restoration company was keying every supplier invoice into a spreadsheet by hand. We built a pipeline that turns an invoice email into a validated register, with one approval step before anything is filed.

Invoice intake, extraction and approval, built with n8n, Gmail and Google Drive

Background

The company runs several active sites. Supplier invoices arrive in every format: typed PDFs from established contractors, and phone photographs of handwritten bills from site. Each one had to be opened, read, keyed into a tracking sheet and forwarded for approval. Different people entered the same fields differently, approvals happened informally over chat, and nothing checked an invoice before it entered the books. In one instance a vendor had billed at 93 per cent completion while payments to date stood at 49 per cent, which is exactly the kind of gap a checked intake process is meant to catch.

The problem

Most invoice-extraction tools assume a clean, machine-readable document. That assumption holds for typed PDFs and fails for photographed handwritten bills. A text-based PDF has a text layer to parse; a photograph has none and has to be read visually. The pipeline therefore needed to tell the two apart at the first step and handle each with the method that suits it.

What we built

A single n8n workflow takes over the moment an invoice email lands.

1
Intake and routing

A Gmail trigger watches the invoice inbox. Every attachment is archived to Google Drive immediately, then classified as a structured PDF or an image.

2
Two extraction paths

PDFs go through text extraction followed by an AI step that must return data in a fixed schema, so field names and types stay consistent. Images go to a vision model, prompted for handwritten and photographed bills.

3
Validation before anything is written

Both paths run checks on the extracted data. A failed check sends an email that says exactly what was wrong, so the problem is fixed the same day rather than discovered at reconciliation.

4
Human approval before filing

Invoices that pass are split into line items, written to an Excel register in Drive and sent for approval by email. Nothing is filed until a person confirms it.

What made it work

Three decisions carried the build. The first was matching the extraction method to the input instead of forcing one tool to cover both. The second was the validation gate: the first version wrote extracted data straight to the register, and the gate is what stops a bad row reaching it. The third was keeping a person in the approval step, which for a process tied to vendor payments is what made the finance team comfortable using it.

The system handles clean PDFs with maximum reliability and photographed invoices with enough validation to catch failures rather than let them through. As image volume grows, the next investment would be tightening schema enforcement on the image path specifically, the one currently carrying the least structural validation. That is a deliberate trade-off, not an oversight: the engineering effort went where the volume is.

Results

Invoices move through the pipeline without anyone typing them in. About 96 per cent of extraction failures are caught at the validation step instead of the register, and every invoice received since go-live has been archived to Drive.

Hospitality Live reporting

Automated MIS reporting for a hotel group

A finance analyst at a multi-property hotel group spent about three hours every morning exporting NetSuite reports and pasting them into one Excel file. The add-on that would connect the two directly was not in the budget. We automated the routine using Microsoft 365 tools the team already had.

NetSuite to Excel automation with Power Automate and SharePoint

Background

Each morning the analyst logged into NetSuite, pulled four reports, copied the figures into a master Excel file and emailed it to twelve department heads. By the time it landed, the data was hours old and the morning was gone. The direct fix, NetSuite's SuiteAnalytics Connect, would have queried the data live into Excel, but the add-on licence ran to several thousand dollars a year for one use case, and the CFO did not approve it.

The problem

The constraint was budget and process, not technology. Anything that required new licences, new tools or a change to how the finance team worked was not going to be approved. The brief became: keep the Excel dashboard the team already knows, and refresh it without anyone exporting anything.

What we built

A zero-cost automation connecting NetSuite to the existing Excel report.

1
Scheduled exports

NetSuite was configured to email four daily report snapshots to a dedicated mailbox on a fixed schedule. This uses a feature the client already had, applied deliberately.

2
The bridge

A Power Automate flow detects those emails, extracts the data and replaces the source file in SharePoint, leaving every formula and pivot table in the dashboard untouched.

3
Same dashboard, current numbers

The finance team keeps opening the same Excel file as always. The replacement happens behind the scenes, so the numbers are simply current when they open it.

What made it work

The licence constraint pushed the build into Microsoft's own ecosystem, which kept the dependency list short: no middleware to renew, and no third-party connector whose pricing can change. Because nothing about the team's routine changed, there was nothing to retrain and no reason to drift back to the old export habit.

Results

The daily export routine is gone, which returns roughly three hours a day to the finance team. Data is now under fifteen minutes old where it used to be hours old, and the additional software spend was nil. Valued at salary rates, the reclaimed analyst time is worth over $12,000 a year.

Construction and heritage restoration Data pipeline

Multi-site construction reporting

Six active construction and restoration sites, six ways of recording the day's work: local spreadsheets, handwritten registers, WhatsApp messages. We left the engineers' emails exactly as they were and built a pipeline that turns them into structured data and a live dashboard.

AI email parsing into Sheets, a SQLite backend and a Streamlit dashboard

Background

Daily progress, material and labour data arrived in whatever format was nearest at hand. Nothing matched, so nothing could be compared. The cost appeared downstream: material lists, labour counts and work-order trackers did not line up between site engineers, vendors, architects and finance, which made billing discrepancies hard to catch and harder to prove, and stretched out audits and payments.

The problem

There was one firm constraint on the field side: site engineers were not going to learn a new tool mid-project. The input therefore stayed as an ordinary email, and the system had to do the structuring on their behalf.

What we built

Four stages take the data from an engineer's inbox to a reconciled record.

1
Capture

Engineers email the day's progress, material usage and labour counts exactly as before. No new app and no form to fill in.

2
Extraction and loading

An AI step parses each email for quantities, materials, labour and site references, whatever the phrasing, and writes the result to Google Sheets. A Python pipeline cleans the entries and normalises them into a SQLite database, so all six sites report on one standard.

3
Dashboard

A Streamlit dashboard shows daily operational metrics to project heads and finance, deployed on Streamlit Cloud with GitHub Actions so updates roll out automatically. The Excel daily-progress trackers ran in parallel during rollout, so nobody had to switch cold.

4
Reconciliation

With one clean dataset, material reconciliation against vendor bills surfaced discrepancies that manual tracking had missed, worth ₹4,50,000 in total. Orders, verified bills and updated payment terms were then tracked under formal data-management SOPs and recorded minutes with key partners.

What made it work

Adoption came from removing work rather than adding it. Engineers changed nothing about how they report, so there was no retraining and nothing to push back against. The first reconciliation that found real money did more for buy-in than any instruction could have.

Results

All six sites now report to a single standard, and a labour dispute that once meant flipping through physical registers is settled with a search. The first proper reconciliation recovered ₹4,50,000 in vendor billing discrepancies, so the pipeline paid for itself on that result alone.

Multi-country operations Approval automation

Vendor bill approval workflow

A client running procurement across several countries approved vendor bills over email, with no enforced chain and no record of who signed off what. We built a multi-level approval workflow inside NetSuite. Approval cycles fell from about five days to under 24 hours.

NetSuite SuiteFlow vendor bill approval workflow

NetSuite SuiteFlow, Vendor Bill Approval Workflow (Global)

Background

Approvers, from supervisors through function heads and department heads to finance, sat in different countries and time zones. Every vendor bill, whether raised directly or from a purchase order, needed sign-offs that depended on its amount, the creator's role and the business unit. None of this was enforced in the system. A bill could land in the wrong inbox, sit over a long weekend and reach finance a week later with no trace of who had seen it. The finance lead's summary was that chasing approvals took more time than the payment work itself.

The problem

The tooling already existed inside NetSuite; the chain simply had not been built in it. The fix was to make the system run the chain, with the route a bill takes depending on its amount, whether a PO exists and who created it.

What we built

A SuiteFlow workflow that runs whenever a vendor bill is created or updated.

1
Requestor and Country Head

The creator's designated approver reviews first; the bill then routes to the Country Head for the relevant subsidiary or location, so the right country's authority is always in the loop.

2
Supervisor, Function Head and Department Head

These levels trigger above set amounts. Lower-value bills skip them, so senior review time is spent where it matters.

3
Finance Tier 1

A final finance check on GL coding, tax mapping and PO match before the bill is approved for payment.

4
Rejection reasons

Any approver at any stage can reject, but only through a step that records the reason on the bill permanently. The reason travels with the record instead of staying in a thread.

What made it work

Each approver receives an email the moment the bill reaches their stage, with a direct link to the record. Chasing stopped being anyone's job, and that is what cut the cycle time. Exclusions for recurring bills and the Europe portfolio were written as conditions, so the workflow does not collide with arrangements that already exist.

Results

Approval cycles run under 24 hours across all time zones, down from about five days. Seven conditionally triggered stages run without intervention, and a bill cannot post until its chain is complete. Every decision carries a timestamp and, where a bill was rejected, a recorded reason.
